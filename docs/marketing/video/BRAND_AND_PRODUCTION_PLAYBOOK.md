# Vssyl Video Brand & Production Playbook

**Creative standard:** Vssyl Video Standard V1.0  
**Accepted:** November 23, 2026, following completion of The Day Doesn't Wait (V15).  
**Classification:** CURRENT creative guidance only. Not an architectural, product-release or legal-rights contract.  
**Precedence:** [Canonical Vssyl brand specification](../../product/12_VSSYL_BRAND.md) and [Product Constitution](../../product/01_PRODUCT_CONSTITUTION.md) prevail over this campaign-specific treatment.

## 1. What the films must communicate

**Core:** Vssyl connects **People, Places, Work and Time** so the people responsible for physical service can see what is expected, what is happening, what changed and what needs attention.

A compelling film should answer a buyer's human question before introducing product nouns: *Will I know how my operation is running, and can my people respond when the plan changes?*

- **People:** who is working, qualified, present, assigned, or covering.
- **Places:** where the responsibility or issue exists, using one recognizable facility.
- **Work:** what is expected or recorded, the actual action taken and who is responsible.
- **Time:** when a responsibility is due, service begins, events actually occur, and the next cycle comes into focus.
- **Disruption and recovery:** employees call off, refrigeration runs warm, deadlines approach; the response is human-led and visible.
- **Historical truth:** confirmed evidence and milestones remain in the history as new service cycles begin; unresolved issues do not disappear with the clock.

The visual conclusion may prominently reveal the four words **People → Places → Work → Time**, one at a time in synchronization with the actual spoken words.

**Marketing expression:** The Day Doesn't Wait closes with **“A clearer way to run the day.”** The primary product positioning in the canonical brand specification remains **“Your operations. All together.”** Use the film line only as a campaign tag unless a separately approved brand change occurs.

## 2. Audience, tone and storytelling

Start with Food & Nutrition managers, facility leadership and frontline supervisors who recognize everyday operational complexity. They do not need a technical architecture lecture.

**Tone:** practical, warm, confident, professional, calm, observant. Never condescending, punitive, alarmist or a generic feature-list commercial. Staff are competent operators; the software supports them rather than making decisions for them.

**Creative emphasis:** about 75% story / human operational meaning and 25% real-world specificity and workflow proof. This does not dictate shot length or whether a video is animated versus live application footage. Deeper product demonstrations can use significantly more real footage.

**Default narrative movement:**

1. Orient the viewer: one place, an exact or recognizable time, people performing actual work.
2. Show the facility's normal rhythm: expected duties, roles, service milestones and deadlines.
3. Introduce a realistic deviation (for example, a call-off or out-of-range refrigerator).
4. Show an authorized human response, with Vssyl reflecting actual information and decisions.
5. Show continuity: the operational day continues; the next service cycle comes into focus.
6. Pull back to the larger connected operation, and finish on the brand message.

Do not require every video to contain all six beats. Short ads might show only one authentic moment and a clear payoff.

**Write like an operator speaks:** “One serving location is suddenly short” is stronger than “coverage exception created.” “The supervisor reviews the gap and reassigns an available employee” is stronger than “workflow automation complete.”

## 3. Film-specific fictional setting

**One master facility, many stories.** Reuse the same fictional long-term-care/institutional facility so the audience recognizes its spatial logic across videos. The first approved direction uses neutral-colored walls, charcoal roofs, natural greens, light interiors, simple cutaway rooms and restrained 2.5D/isometric perspective.

- Food & Nutrition focus: commercial kitchen, refrigerator, ovens, meal preparation, serving stations and dining areas.
- Facility Plant Operations focus: equipment, mechanical spaces, repairs, service-request response and preventive work.
- Environmental Services focus: corridors, resident rooms, housekeeping/storage and routine cleaning responsibilities. Until EVS is AVAILABLE, this is conceptual setting artwork, **not evidence of a customer-ready EVS workflow**.
- Combined view: one facility and a single shared physical hierarchy; no duplicated building for each department.

Scale should be **representative**, not a literal replica of the founder's facility. Maintain stable placement of kitchen, dining, resident areas, loading/access, plant room and corridors; do not regenerate a different building in every shot.

**Asset continuity rules:** keep visual identity consistent for the building silhouette, rooflines, kitchen equipment, important employees, uniforms, work tablets, time readouts and recurring UI cards. New art must be compared to the approved master before use. A flattened PNG is a reference image, not an editable scene rig.

## 4. Visual design

**Environment (2.5D):** isometric/cutaway facility views, restrained perspective changes, soft warm light and realistic proportions simplified for readability.

**Information (flat 2D):** temperature readouts, staff coverage cards, due/confirmed milestones, time bars, alerts, data flow and role views. They overlay the physical world or transition to real application footage.

**Keep the design quiet.** Clear hierarchy, strong readable contrast, no busy infographics, excessive purple, photo-realistic overload, vibrating notifications, wild camera moves or unnecessary technical diagrams.

**Color principles:**

- The approved film uses neutral architecture with accent colors to identify statuses and related functions. Accent colors are not decorative wallpaper.
- The **canonical product palette**, not the video illustration palette, governs actual application footage: brand/hero #0B3D3A, primary accent #0F766E, canvas #E8EEF3, surface white and zinc-900 operational text. See [Brand 12](../../product/12_VSSYL_BRAND.md).
- Existing semantic status colors carry real meaning. Do not show a green confirmation for a condition still unresolved.
- Department accent colors in concept art are optional navigation aids; do not turn them into a new official product color system.

**Type and logo:** follow the approved Vssyl logo/wordmark and Geist brand typography from the canonical brand specification. Do not treat AI-generated approximations or spelling in illustration frames as production-ready logo geometry. Place final on-screen text as editable compositing layers when possible.

## 5. Animation and pacing

The visual hierarchy should communicate the story with sound off. Use subtle pans, restrained slow zooms, soft cutaway reveals, coordinated location highlights and simple card transitions.

- Let **approved narration determine edit timing**. Do not force a natural voice into arbitrary 30/60/90-second targets. Target lengths may be re-edited after the master exists.
- Use exact or representative clock times to establish urgency. If an example uses 7:30 AM breakfast, make clear this is a staged facility configuration, not a universal regulatory deadline.
- Distinguish planned time and actual/confirmed time; “not confirmed” does not mean the work did not occur.
- One operational cycle leads into the next; do not reset yesterday/today or silently close an unresolved issue.
- On the closing frame, animate **People → Places → Work → Time** in discrete, legible reveals matching the narration. Hold the completed set before resolving to the Vssyl end card.
- Leave safe typography margins for captions, 16:9 playback and future square/vertical edits. Check motion legibility at phone size.

The first approved final is a 1280 × 720 / 25 fps H.264 master. That is a historical export fact, **not** a ban on producing higher-resolution future source files.

## 6. Narration and audio

**Approved qualities:** warm, natural American male; approachable, articulate, quietly enthusiastic, not a dramatic announcer and not an overly cheerful corporate voice.

**Brand pronunciation:** **Vssyl** sounds exactly like the English word **“vessel”** (/ˈvɛsəl/). Never read it as initials or guess from the spelling. Use the exact written brand name in display copy; use pronunciation tools/dictionaries in narration generation.

**Narrator reference:** the product owner liked the “Leo — Approachable & Crisp” HeyGen audition and its pronunciation after correction. The first film's later approved final voice asset was supplied by the user as a WAV; this playbook does not assert that its underlying voice model is Leo or grant permission to clone his voice.

**Music:** subtler than a typical SaaS commercial. Slightly upbeat and optimistic, sparse harmony and texture, smooth forward momentum, **well behind the voice**. No foreground melody, prominent drums, strong rhythmic hooks or chimes fighting the words. Let the narrator guide the viewer.

- Do a narration-only listen before adding music.
- Place gentle ambience/music at a low, controlled level; if it draws attention, it is too loud. Duck under speech; do not make a fixed decibel recipe from earlier experiments.
- Do not dramatize equipment problems with alarms or ominous stingers.
- The final four word reveals may breathe without prominent musical hits; a restrained resolution may accompany the end card.
- Preserve a clean voice stem, music stem and mixed master for future language, duration and caption versions.

**Audio rights:** do not assume free AI voice generation, music loops or third-party assets carry unrestricted advertising rights. Before publishing, obtain/check applicable terms, license records, attribution obligations and model-provider restrictions.

## 7. Product truth and decision ownership

Narrative illustrations are **staged examples**, not evidence that software automatically made an operational decision.

**Default refrigerator scenario boundary:**

1. A person checks a refrigerator and records an out-of-range temperature against its configured standard.
2. A required corrective action is documented. The team follows its own food-safety program (assess food temperatures/exposure, hold or move product as appropriate); a high air-temperature reading alone does not prove food safety or require a uniform disposition.
3. An authorized human marks the asset **Out of service** when appropriate. The status change is not automatically caused by a reading.
4. A requester submits a maintenance **Request** through a configured route. An authorized Plant supervisor reviews it; creating a separate **Work Order** is a decision, not a consequence guaranteed by every Request.
5. A completed Work Order does not automatically prove the issue is resolved or that equipment is returned to service. Asset status and operational recovery are separate facts.

**Default call-off boundary:** a shift or attendance change can create an assignment/coverage need; an authorized supervisor may change the assignment. Do not show Vssyl inventing eligible replacements or filling coverage automatically.

**Product release boundary:** Food & Nutrition and Facility Plant Operations are current AVAILABLE examples per [Product Constitution](../../product/01_PRODUCT_CONSTITUTION.md). [EVS remains DEVELOPMENT](../../evs/README.md) and must be clearly framed as a future/conceptual layer, not a current customer feature. Reconfirm release status at production time.

**Other guards:** no invented active-customer results, regulatory certifications, time/cost savings percentages, or outcomes implied by a completed record alone. Marketing must distinguish **implemented and verified**, **implemented with gaps**, **approved target**, **demonstration only**, and **future vision**.

## 8. Evidence and approval gates

For every video, retain:

1. **Concept approval** — audience, goal, narrative, claims and proposed medium.
2. **Script approval** — exact version, transcript, brand pronunciation, staged times and who performs each action.
3. **Art/storyboard approval** — facility continuity, colors, annotations and reuse.
4. **Operational validation** — path and owner for each demonstrated UI action plus current implementation/release status.
5. **Voice approval** — chosen voice/audio take, natural timing, narration file and source/licensing.
6. **Picture/audio review** — real source media, caption timing, correct reveal order, level balance and mobile readability.
7. **Publish gate** — rights/license confirmation, privacy and synthetic-demo disclosure where appropriate, accessibility, retention and export checks.

**Publication is not implied by a product owner's creative approval.** Archive final masters and source files in durable storage and record their paths. The repo stores decisions and manifests, not unreviewed media, customer information or large generated files.

## 9. Video-family guidance

| Type | Typical goal | Typical treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand/overview | Explain why Vssyl exists | Mostly 2.5D story with selective flat overlays |
| Single operational moment | Relatable hook and consequence | A short fictional moment, one clear outcome |
| Workflow story | Show a complete decision and continuity loop | Story first, then illustrative/verified product view |
| Product explainer | Clarify the operating model (Run/Build, roles, cycles) | Flat diagrams + genuine UI + facility |
| Deep dive/demo | Prove actual navigation and actions | Primarily sanitized real screen recordings |
| Founder/human story | Demonstrate lived operational perspective | Human narrative with gentle visuals |

Future examples include *The Call-Off*, *The Refrigerator Isn't Holding Temperature*, *The Manager's Morning*, *Build the Plan. Run the Day.*, *A Day in Food & Nutrition* and later explicitly conceptual EVS exploration. These are ideas, not completed productions or guaranteed roadmap items.

# Harbor Console Phase 1B Facility-scoped assistance

**Status: proposed for architecture and product-owner review.** Phase 1B starts from the Phase 1A merge at [`23c6e6e9`](https://github.com/ADTrauts/LTC-Manager/commit/23c6e6e9b895508ebfc3bf16e8631ec2838459f1). This document proposes the transition, persistence, and certification gates.

## Decision to review

Retire legacy staff admission to customer routes before enabling a new read surface. Place assistance under the Harbor Console with an explicit staff principal. Each protected read must prove a live Harbor login, a live grant for the requested Facility and operation, and a live assistance session that records purpose. Apply the owning domain's resource constraints before accessing data. Start with one approved read of Facility structure. Keep customer credentials, HR, authority transfers, private Records and attachments, billing changes, and sensitive settings outside the initial surface. Clearer labels and fewer links help staff navigate but leave server admission in charge.

The approved policy requires a recorded Facility grant for both OWNER and MEMBER. Grants confer authority within an explicit Facility and operation scope. Staff role, Organization membership, and other domain assignments confer no automatic assistance access. Emergency access requires a separate decision. The [Phase 1 authority architecture](HARBOR_CONSOLE_PHASE_1_STAFF_AUTHORITY_ARCHITECTURE.md) and [Phase 1A certification](HARBOR_CONSOLE_PHASE_1A_CATALOG_PUBLICATION_CERTIFICATION.md) establish the boundary for this slice.

## Current state and dependency map

Work entry checks live Harbor identity and Facility existence. The Harbor work token carries staff and Facility references and expires after 12 hours. The proxy first evaluates the route registry, then admits a valid work token on selected customer path families. The application session reader independently resolves the work context, projects `FACILITY_ADMINISTRATOR`, and gives it precedence over the customer cookie. It can fall back to a separately valid customer session when the work context cannot resolve. Work exit and staff logout clear cookies. Start and exit have audit events, but there is no live grant or session row that makes an already issued work cookie cease to authorize when exit is committed. Existing staff identity, active status, role, and version checks continue to apply.

The static inventory covered 96 source modules and 52 route, page, layout, or Server Action files under the nine legacy allowed prefixes. It also searched for explicit Harbor session consumers beyond those prefixes and traced common session readers to the domains below. Counts describe source entry files, not distinct operations or verified runtime reachability. Feature gates, record state, and different guards affect the behavior of individual operations. Treat every unclassified handler as denied for assistance, including handlers registered by later changes.

## Proposed persistence and lifecycle

Harbor owns the two proposed models. Neither row creates a customer identity or membership. An existing staff account and an existing Facility are referenced by foreign keys. Use the same actors and transaction conventions as the Phase 1A capability. Retain closed rows pending approved retention policy.

| Proposed model | Fields and ownership | Invariants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `HarborFacilityGrant` | `id`, subject `staffId`, `facilityId`, a bounded set of approved read-operation keys, `approvedByStaffId`, `approvedAt`, `startsAt`, `expiresAt`, optional safe approval/consent reference, `revokedAt` and revoker. | The approval service validates the operation keys against the code-owned registry. Each grant identifies one Facility and one staff member. Require nonempty operations and finite half-open validity. Change scope by revoking and issuing a new grant with approval. No legacy backfill. |
| `HarborAssistanceSession` | `id`, `grantId`, non-secret binding to a specific Harbor login, structured purpose and optional safe reference, `startedAt`, `expiresAt`, `endedAt`, end reason and actor when applicable. Derive staff and Facility from the grant. | A session inherits at most the grant's approved operations. Its expiry is at or before both grant and login expiry and the approved session maximum. A session is valid only while the underlying grant and login are also live. Prefer one selected Facility per login initially; confirm that operating policy before adding its partial unique index. |

Use additive Prisma migrations and checked-in PostgreSQL constraints for valid intervals and consistent end/revoke metadata. Index grant history by staff and Facility and sessions by grant and login binding. Avoid cascading deletions that break required evidence. Decide retention and anonymization before opening the feature. Preserve existing Harbor and domain audit ownership. Add typed request/session correlation and safe outcome fields to the existing Harbor event stream where needed, with an approved retention migration if current deletion behavior is incompatible. The migration must work on an empty and a representative disposable copy of the prior schema with existing data retained.

1. **Approve.** Only a product-approved grant approver can create a grant under an explicit staff lifecycle capability. Verify staff and Facility against canonical rows. Protect approval from self-grant and validate any consent using the approved workflow; settle how to handle the single-owner case before enabling grant issuance. Extend the Phase 1A Harbor capability policy with approved approval, entry and read operations as each is admitted.
2. **Start.** Validate the current signed staff login and live role/version/email. Resolve one active grant with the expected Facility, record purpose, and create the session and start event atomically. Bind to a per-login identifier on the signed Harbor session, such as a new `jti`; logins without it must reauthenticate to enter assistance. This uses existing login validation and needs no new staff identity or login table. Scope the work cookie to the assistance surface and issue it only after commit. Browser values carry an opaque or signed session reference with no role or permissions.
3. **Read.** One server resolver checks live staff identity, current login binding, the entry and read capabilities, grant, session, time, and operation. The owning reader applies the resource's authoritative Facility and Department or operator rules. Derive Facility scope from the resolved session and join it to owned rows before selecting details, child lists or aggregates. Deny expired, revoked, unknown, foreign and unclassified requests before projecting data. Return no foreign resource metadata.
4. **End or revoke.** Explicit exit ends the matching session server-side even if the UI has moved elsewhere. Harbor logout ends assistance for that login; grant revocation invalidates every session under that grant. Deactivation, role/session-version change and expiry immediately cut off subsequent protected requests through live checks. Write the end/revoke event in the same transaction. Cookie clearing is cleanup. A failed persistence operation must not be represented as successful server-side termination.
5. **Resume or switch.** If the single-context policy is approved, switching ends the previous session before issuing the new reference. A form or tab still bound to the previous session or Facility fails. Approval and start retries must be safe when a transaction commits but the response is lost. Expiry is enforced in the decision, irrespective of maintenance jobs; a later job may mark expired sessions for history.

## Enforcement and transition

Start with a dedicated Harbor assistance shell and an explicit server-only `HarborAssistanceActor` that cannot satisfy the customer `FacilitySession` union. It carries staff identity, grant, session, authorized Facility, and the requested operation. Keep the route registry's Harbor staff gate as admission to the surface. The owning server reader independently requires the assistance decision. Every route, Server Action and API method must have explicit registration. A dedicated assistance handler does not read or fall back to a customer cookie. As part of legacy removal, reject stale work intent at the proxy and shared entry points, clear the legacy cookie and require deliberate return to the appropriate customer or staff flow. Normal customer navigation still validates the actual customer principal.

The initial read surface has no customer write commands, uploads, file downloads, exports, device binding, offline queues or actor-switching APIs. A GET label is insufficient because existing loaders can initialize data. Use only a reviewed read projection with a narrow query interface and a bounded select. Facility structure is a candidate operation. Reuse canonical Facility/Unit/Space facts and topology helpers while extracting a pure read from the backfilling loader; apply approved field restrictions and resource filters before projection. The stored session and its audit events are permissible governance writes. Scope and expiry decisions must not depend on feature flags for CDA or Records authentication. Use response-level `no-store`, fail closed on authority-store failure, and keep cross-session data out of cache and prefetch state.

To serialize revocation and access, adopt one order for authority locks: live staff row, grant, session, then the owning domain's resource locks. Phase 1A already demonstrates transactional staff validation. A short authorized read may hold shared authority locks through its scoped query and evidence write. Recheck time and authority after waiting, using a current clock, and again at the protected operation boundary. Revocation takes the corresponding write locks and commits state and evidence together. Once revocation commits, subsequent requests must deny, including another browser tab or a retained reference. A revocation cannot recall a response already authorized and issued. Avoid data streams and background reads in the initial slice.

Future assisted writes require a separately approved operation and customer consent policy, a CDA owner classification where applicable, and the real staff actor passed to the domain command. Inside the mutation transaction, recheck live grant/session, expiry and target ownership after lock waits, then commit the mutation and evidence together. The revocation test must prove that a mutation waiting behind a committed revocation leaves no changed state. The first implementation exposes no assisted domain write.

Record actual staff ID and principal kind, target Facility, stable resource reference where appropriate, grant/session, operation, purpose category, outcome, timestamp and request correlation. Log grant approval, start, read, end and revocation; retain redacted denial evidence without revealing foreign resource existence. Use structured codes or an approved reference for sensitive reasons. Avoid free-text disclosures, credentials, cookie values, private payloads, and public log output containing customer details. Decide audit reviewer access and retention before enabling the feature.

## Risk and certification

| Risk | Required control and test |
| --- | --- |
| Legacy role or genuine customer cookie authorizes an assisted request | Retire both legacy mint and role projection. With both customer and staff cookies present, check that assistance uses only its own context and expired/old references deny. Verify normal customer sign-in, role periods, vendor workspace, and user/employee attribution independently. |
| Cross-Facility access via list, detail, search, child references, or aggregates | Use synthetic grants for Facility A and test lists and resources from both A and B. Compare counts, direct handlers, missing versus foreign objects, altered scope and the same URLs with Owner without a grant. Include role changes and Department/operator-period isolation. |
| Replay after end, grant revoke, logout, expiry, account change or switch | Exercise start/end/revoke in a disposable database. Retry requests with old browser references, across tabs and logins, and after expiry and staff changes. Race reads and revocation using real PostgreSQL locks and cover expired periods while waiting. |
| Read-only UI hides a write that remains callable | Test the route registry, Server Actions, each method, and owning service directly, with both CDA flag states. Assert unchanged domain rows and no upload, import, backfill or background task. Test that an approved read succeeds and creation, update, download and unclassified operations deny. |
| Viewer or cached response exposes private Records or attachments | Enforce domain exclusions and response cache policy. Preserve Phase 0 Catalog ownership and Implementation participant/scanner checks on both Support and dedicated readers. Test the Phase 1A capability and authorized ordinary Support and customer paths. |
| Missing evidence or misattributed writes | Verify actor/session correlation, no customer principal attribution, field redaction, and atomic end/revoke. Test absent or failing audit and unavailable authority database. Any later write test must cover rollback, retries and revocation ordering. |

Run hermetic policy and route classification, disposable PostgreSQL isolation and concurrent lifecycle tests, TypeScript, build and protected Preview browser verification with synthetic identities. Verify navigation, legacy exit, session switching and normal customer workflows. Include an internal Facility Administrator, a Department employee, and a scoped partner in the regressions. Require all applicable Verify checks and document exact SHA, scope, test result and remaining evidence gaps. PASS requires evidence for every enabled operation. PASS WITH GAPS is reserved for a limitation outside the enabled boundary with a recorded closure requirement. Any enabled unauthorized read/write, authority loss without effective revocation, or missing required negative test is FAIL.

## Implementation sequence and rollout

| Package | Changes and dependencies | Acceptance before shipping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1B.1 — Retire legacy work admission** | After review, disable legacy work entry and remove legacy work cookies as customer authority at both proxy and application resolution. Keep an explicit cleanup/exit path and preserve Console and customer flows. Inspect Setup Imports, Support/Implementation work guards, and clients that consume the old app context. Requires approval of the temporary assistance pause. No schema change. | A valid old work token cannot authorize a protected read or write. Mixed and invalid sessions fail in the intended context. Normal customer authority, Phase 0 boundaries and Phase 1A publication pass. |
| **1B.2 — Durable grant/session kernel** | Add forward schema, typed grant approval and session operations, existing staff validation, durable request binding, and minimum audit evidence. Ship with the new surface disabled. Requires approver, expiry, consent and retention decisions. | Schema migration from both baseline states, correct interval/owner rules, end/revoke concurrency, no old-token compatibility, and zero generated grants. |
| **1B.3 — One approved read** | Register the Harbor assistance shell and one read-only Facility structure projection. Confirm its fields, scope and excluded data. Enable only for a synthetic Preview pilot with recorded grants. | Two-Facility isolation, negative action and method coverage, read without customer data mutation, real staff attribution, and customer workflows verified via browser and SQL. |
| **1B.4 — Expand by operation after review** | Add approved read projections individually. Remove residual compatibility references after confirming no other consumers need them. Treat future writes, emergency access, broader lifecycle and evidence policy as separate approvals and work. | Each operation has canonical owner checks, direct denial, audit and rollback evidence before enablement. |

Recommend **1B.1** as the first implementation PR. It closes the compatibility admission boundary without depending on grant-policy decisions or adding a replacement bypass. Support and Implementation can continue through their existing authorized domain workflows during the pause. Establish the legacy-disabled release as the rollback floor. Keep the new feature disabled until the protected pilot passes, including any old reachable aliases during rollout. On a failed pilot or release, disable new entry and resolution and invalidate affected sessions server-side; retain forward migrations and evidence. Roll back only to a revision that also denies legacy work authority.

Before coding, approve the temporary assistance pause and the 1B.1 acceptance tests. Before issuing grants or opening the pilot, decide who may approve and revoke grants, how to handle self-approval and single-owner operation, grant/session maximums and renewal, when to require customer consent and how to record it, audit access and retention, whether a login supports one selected Facility, and the exact first read fields.
